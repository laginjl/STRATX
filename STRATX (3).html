STRATX/Thinking

Not more information. Better governance of it.

Precision Care Management (PCM): A Constitutional Framework for Healthcare Governance. Version 1.0, by Dr. Lagin J. Lathis. 173 pages, published on SSRN in August 2026.

Read on SSRN ↗

The problem

Hospitals are not short of data. They are short of governed meaning.

Healthcare organisations measure almost everything, yet leaders still struggle to answer the questions that matter. What is actually happening? Who owns it? What needs attention first? Can a serious failure hide behind good performance elsewhere?

PCM defines governance as the conversion of distributed performance information into accountable decisions, within the finite attention of the people who govern.

How PCM works

The compliance gate

Some things can't be traded away.

In PCM's scoring function, S(u) = 2K(u) + A(u), K is the compliance indicator (1 if no critical failures, 0 if any exist) and A is the weighted aggregate of substitutable domains. Compliant units score in [2,3]; units with critical failures score in [0,1]. The gap between 1 and 2 is absolute. No amount of substitutable strength can bridge it.

Up the hierarchy, compliance is never averaged: a parent is compliant only if every unit beneath it is, K(P) = min K(Ci). One failing ward makes the hospital non-compliant, whatever its other results.

PCM tiers are never reported as a bare number: the pillar profile is always displayed beside any aggregate. That is why the illustration shows A, K and S separately.

Six foundational commitments

01Evidential grounding

Governed decisions must be traceable to objective evidence about performance. The framework rejects governance by assertion.

02Attention conservation

Leaders’ attention is finite, so governance outputs are reduced to what is relevant to decisions rather than everything that can be measured.

03Assigned accountability

Every surfaced decision has an identifiable owner. Analytical systems can inform decisions, but they can never become the accountable decision-maker.

04Auditability

Every score, signal and decision can be reconstructed from its inputs: where the number came from, what evidence produced it, what rule was applied and who made the judgement.

05Falsifiability

PCM applies its own discipline to itself. Its claims must remain capable of being shown wrong, and it states the conditions under which it should be revised or abandoned.

06Primacy of patient welfare

Patient welfare is the terminal criterion. Efficiency, finance and other objectives serve it and can never become unlimited substitutes for it. The paper names this openly as a normative commitment.

Five domains

01Clinical Quality Outcomes

The benefit produced through clinical work and the resulting quality of care.

02Safety and Governance non-substitutable

The prevention of harm, governance breaches and failures of institutional control. A failing state here is a critical failure that no strength elsewhere can offset.

03Operational Efficiency

The economy and effectiveness with which activity is converted into useful output.

04Financial Stability

The organisation’s capacity to remain financially viable and keep operating.

05Patient and Staff Experience

The lived quality of the healthcare encounter and of working inside the organisation.

A framework that cannot say what would prove it worthless cannot claim to be evidence-based.PCM v1.0, Chapter 0
